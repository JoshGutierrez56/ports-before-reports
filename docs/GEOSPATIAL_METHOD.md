# Busy port or bottleneck? Measurement method

## Scope and evidence status

This is a **retrospective descriptive measurement** of NOAA's 2024 AIS broadcast points in three bounded gateway regions. It is separate from H1, the locked PortWatch/container-import forecasting experiment. No 2024 trajectory, attractive map, or measurement sensitivity changes H1's ports, target, features, ridge grid, or scoring. Pilot exposure is recorded in `artifacts/audit/geo_ledger.jsonl`.

The population is an **AIS cargo-vessel proxy** (reported vessel-type codes 70–79), not a verified containership roster. It excludes reported tankers, tugs, ferries, fishing and recreational traffic; unknown categories remain unknown in retained source crops and are not silently included as freight. A ship position does not measure loaded TEUs or prove an import, delay, cargo destination, or reason for stopping.

The accepted measurement covers **366 source days, January 1–December 31, 2024**, with **12,408,103 cleaned cargo-proxy points**, **23,986 candidate episodes**, **1,098 gateway-day summaries** and **21 gateway/scenario sensitivity rows**. Its actual dates, counts and hashes are in `artifacts/audit/geo_measurement_receipt.json`, linked to the completed `geo_finalization_state.json`. All 366 retained source-crop hashes were independently replay-verified after acceptance. This is full-year source-partition coverage, not proof that every vessel was detected. The immutable 14-day pilot decision is `artifacts/audit/geo_pilot_freeze.json`; the 60 reviewed trajectory examples remain pilot evidence, not a fresh full-year review sample.

## NOAA product, availability and bounded acquisition

Source: [NOAA 2024 broadcast-point documentation](https://github.com/ocm-marinecadastre/ais-vessel-traffic/blob/main/data/ais-broadcast-points-2024-readme.md). The product is GeoParquet 1.0, Snappy, WKB Point, WGS84, UTC, downsampled to minute-level frequency, with a product-specific **CC0 1.0** statement. Original timestamps are preserved rather than assumed rounded. The citation is Martin, Brass, Dornback and Fontenault (2025), *Nationwide Automatic Identification System 2024*, NOAA Office for Coastal Management; acknowledgements US Coast Guard Navigation Center.

January 1 metadata was verified live: 7,293,408 national rows, seven row groups, 227,181,435 bytes. The live source has `mmsi`, `base_date_time`, `sog`, `cog`, heading, reported vessel type, dimensions, vessel-name fields and WKB geometry, not separate source latitude/longitude columns. The crop derives longitude/latitude from verified WKB Point encoding. Source CRS is checked before reading coordinates. A source hash, ETag, Last-Modified, actual transferred bytes, source/cropped row counts, per-gateway counts, crop hash and schema hash are retained per day in `data/manifests/noaa_points.jsonl`.

The January 1 object was last modified June 20, 2025. The 2026 retrieval date and that object modification do **not** establish availability in 2024. There is no contemporaneous public-feed claim. Related derived track products are not substituted for timed point observations.

The first suffix-Range probe unexpectedly received HTTP 200 and consumed 227,181,435 bytes before the intended pilot gate. This mistake is recorded in the append-only ledger and included in transfer accounting. The implemented adapter now checks HTTP 206 and the exact Content-Range **before consuming a resumed response**, uses explicit range endpoints for metadata, and has an adversarial regression test. It never prints or publishes source binary data.

Acquisition is sequential-provider, at most one request start per second, with a single managed prefetch worker overlapping a single-threaded crop. A bounded national cache retains the representative first file and at most the current/prefetched files. Other national files are evicted only from the explicitly resolved project cache after crop row reconciliation and SHA-256 verification. Cropped raw observations are retained. Downloads check a 16 GiB raw-cache cap, 80 GiB project cap, 20 GiB free-disk reserve and cumulative network cap; 2 GiB of the 200 GiB network budget is reserved for other sources, with the parent additionally responsible for the global sum. Interrupted files resume only against the same ETag and declared size. Azure Content-MD5 and SHA-256 are verified. Changed retained input hashes fail rather than silently replacing a vintage.

## Geography and provenance

`data/geography/geofences_v1.geojson` is the measurement geometry, with a hash-linked provenance file. The rectangle for each gateway is only an **acquisition/approach envelope**, never a queue or harbor definition.

- **San Pedro Bay:** LA and Long Beach share one gateway. Official NOAA designated commercial/general anchorages under 33 CFR 110.214 are spatially selected and unioned; overlaps cannot duplicate vessel-hours. Naval/special recreational areas are excluded from candidate anchorage geometry.
- **New York/New Jersey:** NOAA designated anchorages under 33 CFR 110.155 are used; special recreational anchorage provisions are excluded. The physical crop is not proof of exact equivalence with the port authority's economic reporting perimeter.
- **Savannah:** NOAA's designated-anchorage layer contains a small river location, but [Coast Pilot 4, chapter 7, paragraph 143](https://nauticalcharts.noaa.gov/publications/coast-pilot/files/cp4/CPB4_C07_WEB.pdf) says vessels generally anchor north/northeast of Tybee V-AIS T (31°57′52″N, 80°43′10″W), recommends no anchoring within two nautical miles, and says the river has no anchorage except in emergency. Accordingly, a clearly labeled analyst-interpreted offshore sector excludes a 3,704 m buoy radius; slow river vessels do not automatically become queue candidates. This bounded sector/crop can miss farther-offshore holding. A zero in the modeled area does **not** establish no Savannah queue.

The NOAA [Designated Anchorage Areas service](https://coast.noaa.gov/arcgis/rest/services/OceanReports/DesignatedAnchorageAreas/MapServer/0) supplies actual polygons, original IDs/names/CFR references and query counts. Its source CRS is Web Mercator; extraction requests EPSG:4326. Current geometry vintage is not verified as the 2024 boundary vintage.

### Berth refinement during the pilot

Initial hand-locator quay corridors under-covered some visibly stationary berth-side trajectories. Before freeze, they were supplemented/reconciled with actual [NOAA ENC Direct harbor geometry](https://encdirect.noaa.gov/arcgis/rest/services/encdirect/enc_harbour/MapServer), **without changing speed, persistence or gap thresholds**:

- LA/LB and Garden City add ENC coastline lines (layer 84), selected within 600 m of Coast-Pilot-attributed terminal locators and buffered 150 m in the local projected CRS. Full server counts reconciled: 133 LA/LB coastline features and 52 in the bounded Garden City terminal request. Because this layer omits artificial quay edges in several locations, the explicitly approximate interpreted corridors are retained, not falsely called surveyed boundaries.
- NY/NJ replaces named-terminal hand locators with ENC facility perimeter geometry (layer 179, five returned features), while retaining an interpreted Red Hook corridor. Port Elizabeth, Global Marine and New York Container Terminal receive container-berth candidate attribution from the Coast Pilot descriptions; Port Newark is explicitly mixed/other commercial berth. ENC's `CATHAF` values incorrectly label some named facilities ferry/yacht terminals, so that field is **not** treated as container-class evidence. Facility geometry source/chart dates are preserved.
- The initial geometry and initial sensitivity report remain preserved. Incomplete, transfer-limited broad coastline extracts were diagnostic references only, not adopted geometry. Dedicated berth line/area layers returned no features in the selected regions and were not invented.

These are **candidate berth corridors**, not legal terminal ownership or exact mooring polygons. Current ENC outlines and old chart dates can miss terminal expansion. Functional selection near neighboring facilities remains approximate. Geometry sensitivity is part of the result, not an uncertainty hidden by a polished map. Coast Pilot sources: [volume 7 chapter 4](https://nauticalcharts.noaa.gov/publications/coast-pilot/files/cp7/CPB7_C04_WEB.pdf), [volume 2 chapter 11](https://nauticalcharts.noaa.gov/publications/coast-pilot/files/cp2/CPB2_C11_WEB.pdf), and volume 4 chapter 7. These describe facility function and navigation; they do not independently validate every inferred stop.

Local meter calculations use UTM 11N (San Pedro Bay), 18N (NY/NJ), and 17N (Savannah). Geographic-degree buffers are rejected by a test. Geometry is published in WGS84. Transit-channel interpretations override anchorage membership; berth geometry overrides transit. Every observation receives at most one modeled zone and one gateway.

## Cleaning and state continuity

Timestamps are timezone-aware UTC. Points are sorted across all source dates by vessel and time, so midnight and monthly boundaries never reset a continuing state. Exact repeated observations are removed. Conflicting positions/speeds at the same identifier/timestamp are rejected rather than averaged. Coordinate and identifier ranges are checked. Speed 102.3 knots and invalid speed/course/dimension values become unknown. Implied jumps above 60 knots create explicit UNKNOWN markers and do not authorize filling the missing track.

The causal raw state is approach/transit, low-speed anchorage candidate, low-speed berth candidate, or unknown. Candidate status requires **30 minutes of already observed persistence** at speed no greater than **1 knot**. Before that it is `PERSISTENCE_PENDING`. Moving through an anchorage is transit, not waiting. Boundary/speed jitter resets persistence instead of producing repeated confirmed calls. The engine reconstructs candidate-zone entry/departure, not a complete voyage census: disappearance beyond the acquisition bounds is censored rather than a fabricated departure. A future extension can add externally verified full harbor entry boundaries.

Between-ping occupancy is attributed only when both endpoint classifications agree, persistence has already elapsed, and the gap is at most **30 minutes**. The first persistence interval is not retrospectively credited. The bounded hold assumption is recorded on every interval; it is not an observed position at every intermediate instant. Longer gaps are unobserved, not extended dwell. Retrospective intervals split exactly at UTC midnight with conserved duration. The daily as-of summary additionally excludes a prior-day interval portion that can only be established by a ping at/after the exclusive day-end cutoff. This deliberately leaves a small unallocated boundary interval instead of borrowing future observations.

Full episodes contain observed bounds, duration, source linkage and left/right censor flags. Completed duration requires an already observed transition out of the state without a long gap. A first-seen stationary ship is left censored: its elapsed observed age is a lower bound. Future completion information is kept in episode QA, never substituted into an earlier active-age feature. Daily censor fractions are retrospective QA fields, not forecast predictors.

## Daily units and coverage

- `observed_arrivals`: distinct eligible vessels first confirming a persistent candidate-zone episode after an observed preceding state. These are **candidate-zone arrivals**, not a complete count of port calls. First-seen left-censored arrivals are excluded.
- `anchorage_hours`, `berth_hours`: conservative bounded-interval candidate vessel-hours, never scaled up to a full day.
- `queue_stock`: observed anchorage candidates with a last ping within the maximum gap of UTC day end, only on adequately represented days. It is not all ships waiting or verified delayed containerships.
- `median_active_age_hours`: median elapsed observed candidate age through the last admissible ping, not future completed waiting duration. Zero is used only alongside a verified zero-*observed-candidate* indicator in the modeled area.
- Completed berth medians require observed departure by the cutoff and exclude left/right-censored episodes.
- Source completeness, eligible ping counts, hours of day represented, ping-interval continuity, invalid-record counts and censoring accompany the measures.

A complete downloaded source day is not proof that all vessels were detected. Queue stock/age requires all 24 UTC hourly buckets represented and at least 80% of consecutive eligible ping intervals within the permitted gap. These are engineering coverage proxies, not receiver-uptime estimates or a percentage of all ships observed. Sparse/unknown days get null stock and age; missing source partitions are null, not zero. Spatial truncation and undocumented receiver coverage can understate candidate stock even on a temporally adequate day.

## Friction and sensitivity

The optional friction summary equally weights robust standardized `log1p(anchorage_hours)` and `log1p(active_age)` against the **previous 90 valid observation days**, requiring at least 30 valid previous days. The current day is excluded. Scale is 1.4826×MAD; zero MAD falls back to prior standard deviation, then 1.0, with a recorded flag. It is not a seasonally adjusted index and is never backfilled into 2019–2023. Raw units remain available.

The frozen one-factor measurement sensitivities are buffers **0/100/250 m**, speeds **0.5/1/1.5 knots**, and maximum gaps **15/30/60 minutes**. They are reported in `geo_sensitivity.csv` separately from H1. No favorable scenario replaces the baseline. Strong berth-buffer sensitivity limits absolute dwell interpretation even if anchorage/low-speed discrimination is visually plausible.

## Actual-data review and external consistency

The audit deterministically selects 20 vessel-day examples per gateway, round-robin across anchorage, berth, transit, boundary/speed jitter, missing intervals and left/right day censoring where those strata actually occur. It does not invent Savannah anchorage examples when none are observed in the pilot. The 60 examples are **not 60 independent vessels or expert validations**. Every map is drawn from actual NOAA positions; no line is drawn across a large observation gap. Individual identifiers are absent from public map/cell exports. Local raw sources retain identifiers for reproducibility.

`geo_trajectory_audit.csv` gives map paths, selected strata, observed point counts, flags and review notes. The review is **agent-assisted**, with explicit visual-review status; neither the user nor an independent maritime expert is claimed as reviewer. Sixty map panels and five contact sheets support inspection. Agent visual checks establish implementation consistency and reveal missed boundary coverage; they cannot establish the true reason for a stop.

BTS/MARAD operational waiting-stock observations are paired on actual matching dates only. The accepted comparison has 51 matched dates per available gateway, with **49 valid San Pedro Bay pairs** and **50 valid Savannah pairs** after excluding missing values. San Pedro Bay's mean modeled candidate stock is **3.29**, versus **0.90** reported waiting ships; Savannah's is **0.00**, versus **4.32**. These material differences are reported, not presented as agreement. Their reported containership population, drift/holding footprint and unspecified time of day differ from these cargo-proxy bounded midnight candidates. Shared AIS/operational origins and definition mismatch make this a descriptive consistency check, not independent ground truth. Savannah's disagreement reinforces the bounded area's coverage limitation: modeled zero does not establish no queue. The matched rows are in `geo_operational_comparison.csv` and the exact summary is linked in `geo_build_acceptance_receipt.json`. The unavailable 2024 berth-duration dashboard is not replaced by a fabricated benchmark.

## Reproduction

The public function interfaces are `audit_noaa(root, config)`, `acquire_ais(root, config, pilot=True/False)`, `build_geospatial(root, config, pilot=True/False)`, and `freeze_pilot(root, config)`. The parent CLI wraps them. Geometry refinement is permitted only before the pilot lock; source resumes verify crops instead of redownloading them.

Executed checks are recorded, not assumed: `python -m pytest tests/test_geo_engine.py tests/test_noaa_acquisition.py -q`. The suite covers channel transit, persistence, jitter, shared LA/LB assignment, UTC duration splitting, missing gaps, censoring, degree-buffer rejection, duplicate/conflicting records, sentinel speeds, impossible jumps, sparse/missing-day nulls, future append invariance, prior-only friction, ignored HTTP ranges and hash changes. Empirical acquisition/build receipts are separate from unit-test success.

### Post-freeze implementation corrections and execution identity

The pilot geometry, numerical thresholds, reviewed audit, and H1 design remain frozen. Code review after the lock identified a censoring edge case: after an invalid-position or unknown-speed marker, the next candidate episode reset persistence but was not marked left censored when the timestamp separation was short. The corrected engine marks that episode left censored. This affects candidate-arrival and completed/censored-episode metadata, not the geometry, bounded candidate hours, queue stock, active observed age, or friction formula. A regression test exercises the invalid-marker transition.

A second conservative edge-case correction requires valid position and known speed for an hourly bucket to count as represented. An entire day of UNKNOWN markers cannot establish zero queue; a regression test verifies null stock in that case. Daily retained-invalid observation counts/rates accompany coverage, with their denominator explicitly limited to retained eligible observations (essential-invalid/deduplicated/conflicting rows removed during cleaning are separately counted in the cleaning receipt). This run has no earlier full-year measurement against which to quantify a correction effect; no zero-effect claim or fabricated before/after comparison is made.

The original managed acquisition worker had imported the earlier implementation, but exited after 265 of 366 source partitions, before any full-year measurement existed. Its helper correctly rejected acceptance with `Initial full-year measurement has not completed`. The exit cause is not established; preserved failure evidence and its hashes are linked in `artifacts/audit/geo_resume_current_status.json`. The authorized one-shot recovery resumed verified crops with current code, acquired all 366 days and built the **first full-year measurement** in a fresh process. Canonical acceptance completed on October 15, 2026 at **21:43:52 UTC**, followed by a separate all-crop/artifact verification at **21:44:41 UTC**. It did not rebuild nonexistent legacy full-year outputs or create a historical correction receipt. The pilot freeze, reviewed pilot trajectories, and earlier pilot geometry/measurement artifacts remain separate historical evidence.

Successful current-code acceptance establishes completion of this measurement stage. Its measurement receipt records executed measurement-module hashes, exact source snapshot identities, protocol/pilot linkage, configured caps, and current-process resource observations. The linked `geo_build_acceptance_receipt.json` states that legacy outputs were not rebuilt. A legacy correction procedure remains available for a different run that genuinely has earlier full-year outputs: it preserves those outputs before rebuilding and records actual changes in `geo_postfreeze_correction_receipt.json`. That conditional procedure is not this run's provenance.

The acquisition retry implementation was also corrected after launch: both numeric and HTTP-date `Retry-After` deadlines are honored without shortening, missing/malformed headers use jittered exponential backoff, permanent HTTP errors are not retried, and HEAD requests have bounded retries. Mocked 429 tests cover both forms. The interrupted original worker used its previously imported retry implementation; the recovery process uses current code. Recorded ledger events distinguish actual acquisition activity, without claiming the newer implementation executed retroactively.

Transfer accounting includes the inadvertently consumed initial national response. Known response-body bytes are distinguished from a 1 MiB conservative reserve for small early discovery responses whose lengths were not recorded. That reserve is not an invented measured byte count. HTTP headers/TLS overhead are not response-body measurements. All national-file transfer bodies, retained source/crop sizes, and per-day hashes are measured explicitly.

Legacy correction first requires a completed initial full-year receipt and matching daily-output hash, and budgets all preservation copies before writing. Current-code acceptance instead verifies the first full-year outputs directly and checks the frozen previous-valid-day friction rule. In either case, `geo_finalization_state.json` must be `completed`, with matching measurement, daily, site, mode-specific acceptance evidence, code, pilot and reviewed-audit hashes. A merely written 366-day output is not sufficient acceptance evidence.

The optional friction implementation was corrected to match frozen protocol 9.5 exactly: its reference sample is the previous 90 valid observation days, not valid days within a 90-calendar-day window. No current or future observation enters it. A sparse-history regression distinguishes these definitions. The 14-day pilot has no estimated friction values. Acceptance verified that the first full-year measurement satisfies the corrected frozen rule; no earlier full-year friction result exists in this run to compare or replace.

### Accepted replay and fresh builds

The canonical `python -m scsi.cli build-geospatial --config configs/project.yaml` command now distinguishes three cases. A completed accepted build is verified and returned unchanged, with no network request or measurement rerun. Existing legacy full-year outputs go through the preserved-output correction procedure above. A genuinely fresh build using the current corrected implementation receives `acceptance_mode: current_implementation_no_legacy_rebuild` and a `geo_build_acceptance_receipt.json`; it does not fabricate prior buggy outputs or a historical correction. Because the original worker exited before full-year measurement, this run used the current-implementation route. Its actual linked receipts passed acceptance and a separate read-only verification, recorded in `geo_independent_acceptance_verification.json`.

Accepted replay checks all 366 retained crop sizes and hashes, source-manifest and snapshot identities, 1,098 unique complete gateway-day rows, frozen settings and geometry, the 60 reviewed pilot rows, protocol lineage, executed measurement code, and linked receipt/daily/site/preservation hashes. A changed accepted input or output raises an error instead of silently rebuilding and erasing the accepted evidence. Both acceptance modes link their actual acceptance evidence through `acceptance_receipt_path` and `acceptance_receipt_sha256` in the measurement and finalization-state receipts.

### Resource execution observation

The original managed acquisition process had 30 allocated OS threads when inspected at 20:34 UTC, including idle native-library pools. Only three threads had accumulated more than one second of CPU time; this observation is not a claim that the total OS-thread count was eight or fewer. Its working set was approximately 0.48 GiB and lifetime peak approximately 0.54 GiB at that check. The worker was reversibly restricted to eight permitted logical CPUs for the remaining run; the prior and enforced affinity masks and per-thread CPU observations are retained in `geo_worker_resource_enforcement.json`. No machine, OpenClaw, or scheduler configuration was changed.

Fresh measurement execution sets BLAS/OpenMP/NumExpr computational pools to one, Arrow CPU workers to at most four and I/O workers to one, and process CPU affinity to at most eight permitted logical CPUs (also capped at half detected logical CPUs). The finalizer launcher applies environment limits before Python imports. Receipts separately report allocated OS-thread counts, Arrow pools, process affinity, measured memory, and configured budgets rather than treating configuration values as measured resource use.

The completed fresh measurement reports **999.64 seconds** of build time. Across the one-shot acquisition recovery and measurement, minute-spaced controller samples observed a maximum worker-tree RSS of **8.71 GiB** and minimum free disk of **63.70 GiB**. These are sampled observations, not a continuous lifetime-peak certificate or the entire project's resource peak; the underlying samples are retained in `geo_resume_resource_samples.jsonl`. The independent post-acceptance verification took 3.69 seconds and did not rerun measurement or forecasting.
